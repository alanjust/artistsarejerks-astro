## Reference Collections — United States

### Peabody Museum of Archaeology and Ethnology, Harvard University
Holds the Swarts Ruin collection — one of the largest systematically documented Classic Mimbres assemblages. Primary reference for Classic Mimbres Style III ceramics. Also holds material from early Southwest excavations across multiple traditions.

### Maxwell Museum of Anthropology, University of New Mexico (Albuquerque)
Strong regional focus across all Southwest traditions. Best single-institution collection for comparative Southwest material. Well-documented provenience on most holdings.

### Arizona State Museum, University of Arizona (Tucson)
The primary institutional repository for Arizona archaeology. Major Hohokam, Salado, and southern Arizona collections. Holds the largest documented collection of Hohokam shell ornaments. Haury's Snaketown material is here.

### Museum of Northern Arizona (Flagstaff)
Specializes in Colorado Plateau and northern Arizona material. Strong Ancestral Puebloan, Hopi, and Sinagua collections. Key reference for northern Arizona ceramic traditions.

### Natural History Museum of Utah (Salt Lake City)
Major Ancestral Puebloan and Utah archaeology collections. Strong basketry and perishable artifact holdings from dry cave contexts.

### Amerind Foundation (Dragoon, Arizona)
Focused collection of Southwest and Mexican archaeological material. Strong on Casas Grandes/Paquimé traditions and northern Mexico archaeology.

### Smithsonian Institution (National Museum of Natural History, Washington DC)
Broad Southwest holdings across traditions. Some provenience issues in older collections but significant material from all major traditions.

### Chaco Culture National Historical Park (New Mexico)
In-situ site collection and museum. Key reference for Chaco-affiliated ceramic and architectural material.

### Mesa Verde National Park (Colorado)
In-situ site collection. Key reference for Mesa Verde Ancestral Puebloan material (Classic period, 1100–1300 CE).

## Key Sites by Tradition

### Mimbres / Mogollon
- **NAN Ranch Ruin** (Grant County, NM) — Most systematically excavated Mimbres site; Shafer production-sequence benchmark
- **Swarts Ruin** (Grant County, NM) — Largest published Classic Mimbres ceramic assemblage; Cosgrove report (1932)
- **Cameron Creek Village** (Grant County, NM) — Excavated 1923–1928; important figurative ceramic assemblage
- **Galaz Ruin** (Grant County, NM) — Anyon/LeBlanc report; social organization reference
- **Harris Village** (Grant County, NM) — Early Mogollon Pithouse period; pre-Mimbres baseline
- **SU Site** (Catron County, NM) — Martin excavations; important for Mogollon Pithouse sequence

### Hohokam
- **Snaketown** (Maricopa County, AZ) — Type site for Hohokam; Haury excavations 1930s and 1960s; primary reference for Classic Hohokam ceramic and shell ornament sequences
- **Casa Grande** (Pinal County, AZ) — Late Hohokam monumental architecture; important for understanding Classic period Hohokam social complexity
- **Pueblo Grande** (Phoenix, AZ) — Platform mound site; well-documented Classic Hohokam assemblage

### Ancestral Puebloan (Anasazi)
- **Pueblo Bonito** (Chaco Canyon, NM) — Primary reference for Chaco-affiliated architecture and material culture; massive turquoise cache; cylinder vessels; macaw remains
- **Cliff Palace / Spruce Tree House** (Mesa Verde, CO) — Classic Mesa Verde cliff dwelling; reference for 13th-century Ancestral Puebloan domestic assemblages
- **Basketmaker II cave sites** (Four Corners region) — Primary reference for early Ancestral Puebloan perishable material: baskets, sandals, fiber artifacts, atlatls
- **Antelope House** (Canyon de Chelly, AZ) — Exceptional textile and perishable preservation

### Casas Grandes / Paquimé
- **Paquimé** (Chihuahua, Mexico) — The largest late prehistoric site in the U.S. Southwest / northern Mexico region; distinctive polychrome ceramics, macaw pens, copper bells, shell ornaments; Minnis/Whalen excavations
- **Joyce Well** (Grant County, NM) — Northern outlier of Casas Grandes sphere; useful for understanding geographic reach

### Salado
- **Besh-Ba-Gowah** (Globe, AZ) — Tonto-Salado cliff dwelling; reference for Salado Polychrome ceramic assemblages
- **Gila Cliff Dwellings** (Grant County, NM) — Late Mogollon/Salado; includes perishable material

## Canonical Object Types by Material Category

### Ceramic Vessels — Mimbres Tradition
- **Classic Mimbres Style III figurative bowl** — Black-on-white, interior figural program, coil-built; the primary reference object for Mimbres analysis. Swarts collection is the baseline.
- **Classic Mimbres Style III geometric bowl** — Same construction, geometric program; technically demanding due to symmetry requirements.
- **Boldface Black-on-White (Style II)** — ~1000–1050 CE; bold geometric, thick lines; earlier than figurative tradition.
- **Three Circle Red-on-White (Style I)** — ~550–1000 CE; beginning of Mimbres decorated ceramic tradition.

### Ceramic Vessels — Hohokam Tradition
- **Hohokam Red-on-Buff** — Buff paste, red painted geometric designs; distinctive "life forms" (birds, lizards, humans); coil-built and paddle-and-anvil finished.
- **Salado Polychrome** (Gila, Tonto, Pinto) — Post-Classic horizon pottery; red, white, and black on buff; widely distributed suggesting ideological spread.

### Ceramic Vessels — Ancestral Puebloan
- **Chaco Black-on-White** — Fine paste, complex geometric hatching; hallmark of Chaco-affiliated production (~900–1150 CE)
- **Mesa Verde Black-on-White** — White slip, black paint, distinctive geometric banding; ~1100–1300 CE
- **Sikyatki Polychrome** — Late prehistoric/early historic Hopi; naturalistic bird imagery; important for Kachina cult iconographic continuity

### Non-Ceramic Objects — Reference Types

#### Carved Organic Objects
- **Southwest wood effigies (various traditions)** — Carved from cottonwood root, pine, or other regional woods; painted and often with fiber/inset attachments; primarily survive in dry cave contexts; mountain lion, bear, and human effigies are common across multiple traditions.
- **Hopi / Pueblo prayer sticks (pahos)** — Cottonwood sticks wrapped with cord and painted; recorded from ethnographic period with strong archaeological analogues in dry cave material; functional and iconographic parallels to prehistoric equivalents.
- **Basketmaker atlatls and dart foreshafts** — Carved wood, often painted; Four Corners dry caves (Broken Roof Cave, etc.); primary reference for Basketmaker-period carved wood technology.

#### Shell Artifacts
- **Hohokam Glycymeris bracelets** — The most diagnostic Hohokam shell artifact type; frog effigies carved on the umbo (hinge area) are a specific sub-type with wide distribution. Shell sourced from Gulf of California. Manufacture debris at Snaketown establishes this as a Hohokam production specialty.
- **Spondylus shell** — Red/orange spiny oyster; Gulf of California and Gulf of Mexico sources; trade material with documented ritual significance; appears in Pueblo Bonito cache contexts, Casas Grandes, and various high-status deposits.
- **Shell trumpets** (Strombus/Turbinella) — Conch and whelk trumpets; found across Southwest in ritual contexts; sourced from Pacific and Gulf coasts.

#### Composite / Ritual Objects
- **Pueblo Bonito turquoise cache** — The largest prehistoric turquoise deposit in North America; thousands of pieces of worked turquoise from Room 33; primary reference for turquoise as ritual/prestige accumulation.
- **Cave cache assemblages (Mogollon Highlands)** — Dry cave deposits including wood effigies, painted wood tablets, fiber bundles, and organic materials absent from open sites. No single definitive "canonical" cache but documented across multiple sites.
- **Medicine bundles (comparative ethnographic record)** — While not directly archaeological, recorded medicine bundle contents from Pueblo and other Southwest traditions provide interpretive context for multi-material ritual assemblages.

## Reference Publications (Non-Ceramic Focus)

### Haury, Emil W. — *The Hohokam: Desert Farmers and Craftsmen* (1976)
The Snaketown excavation report. Primary reference for Hohokam ceramic, shell, and material culture sequences. Haury's typology and chronology remain the baseline for Hohokam analysis.

### Minnis, Paul E. and Michael E. Whalen — *Casas Grandes and the Ancient Southwest* (2015)
Current synthesis of Paquimé archaeology. Replaces Di Peso's earlier multi-volume report as the working reference for Casas Grandes material culture and regional context.

### Schaafsma, Polly — *Indian Rock Art of the Southwest* (1980)
The foundational survey of Southwest rock art iconography. Essential for placing ceramic and portable object imagery within the broader visual tradition. Documents what symbols appear where, when, and in what combinations.

### Cordell, Linda S. — *Archaeology of the Southwest* (1997, 2nd ed.)
The standard synthetic overview. Use as a first reference for placing any artifact in regional and temporal context before moving to tradition-specific literature.

### Crown, Patricia L. — *Ceramics and Ideology: Salado Polychrome Pottery* (1994)
Key work for interpreting post-Classic polychrome ceramics and the Salado horizon. Demonstrates how ceramic conventions carry ideological content across broad geographic areas.

## Digital Repositories

### tDAR — The Digital Archaeological Record
**URL:** https://core.tdar.org
**Access:** Free account required to download files. Registration open to researchers.
**Maintained by:** Arizona State University Digital Antiquity program.
**What's here:** The primary digital repository for Southwest archaeology gray literature — CRM site reports, excavation datasets, GIS data, unpublished field notes, and image collections that never entered formal publication. Significant holdings for Mimbres, Hohokam, Ancestral Puebloan, and Casas Grandes traditions. Many documents inaccessible anywhere else. Search by site name, tradition, county, or keyword.
**For image acquisition:** Site reports frequently include artifact photographs, drawings, and ceramic type plates. Search for site names in the canonical sites list above to find associated documents.
**Limitation:** Image quality varies by document age. Older scanned reports may have low-resolution plates.

## Image Acquisition Guide

This section is operational, not scholarly. It maps where to find artifact images to upload to the analysis tool. The tool works only with images you supply — it has no live database connection. Your job is acquiring the image; the tool's job is analyzing it.

### Tier 1 — Open Access, No Login Required

**Smithsonian Open Access**
URL: https://www.si.edu/openaccess
License: CC0 — no restrictions. High-resolution downloads available directly.
Coverage: All Smithsonian museums including NMNH. Strong Southwest holdings across traditions. Search by culture, material, or object type.
Best for: Broad Southwest coverage. NMNH holds significant Mimbres, Hohokam, and Ancestral Puebloan material.

**Wikimedia Commons**
URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org
License: Varies — most museum uploads are CC or public domain.
Coverage: Uneven but substantial. Search "Mimbres pottery", "Hohokam ceramic", "Ancestral Puebloan" for usable results.
Best for: Quick access to well-known objects that have been widely published and digitized.

**Internet Archive**
URL: https://archive.org
License: Public domain for pre-1927 publications.
Coverage: Full text of Cosgrove's Swarts Ruin report (1932) with ceramic plates. Early Fewkes reports on Hopi and Ancestral Puebloan material. Image quality varies by scan.
Best for: Classic Mimbres Style III ceramics from the Swarts collection plates.

### Tier 2 — Free Account Required

**tDAR — The Digital Archaeological Record**
See Digital Repositories section above.
Best for: Gray literature, unpublished reports, images from specific excavated sites with full provenience documentation.

**JSTOR**
URL: https://www.jstor.org
Access: Free account allows limited monthly article access.
Coverage: American Antiquity, Kiva, Southwestern Lore, Journal of Archaeological Science. Articles frequently include ceramic photographs and drawings.
Best for: Images of specific object types in peer-reviewed contexts with scholarly description.

### Tier 3 — Museum Portals, Variable Access

**Arizona State Museum — University of Arizona**
URL: https://statemuseum.arizona.edu/collections
Coverage: Primary institutional repository for Arizona archaeology. Major Hohokam collections. Shell ornament holdings. Haury's Snaketown material.
Status: Online access improving. Check portal for downloadable images.

**Maxwell Museum — University of New Mexico**
URL: https://maxwellmuseum.unm.edu/collections
Coverage: Strong regional Southwest coverage across traditions. Well-documented provenience.
Status: Not all holdings online. May require direct contact for specific objects.

**Museum of Northern Arizona**
URL: https://musnaz.org/collections
Coverage: Colorado Plateau and northern Arizona. Strong Ancestral Puebloan, Hopi, Sinagua.
Status: Limited online image access. Research library access available by request.

### Tier 4 — Restricted

**Peabody Museum — Harvard University**
URL: https://www.peabody.harvard.edu/collections
Coverage: The Swarts Ruin collection — the single most important Classic Mimbres reference assemblage. Its limited digital access is a significant gap for this tool.
Status: Not a practical source for casual image acquisition. Formal researcher access required. See access strategies below.

### Access Strategies for Restricted Collections

**Casual researcher path:**
Email the registrar or collections manager directly. Identify the specific objects you need (by accession number if possible) and state your purpose. Museums frequently provide low-resolution images for educational or research use on request, even when their online portal is unhelpful.

**For Peabody specifically:**
The Cosgrove report (Swarts Ruin, 1932) on Internet Archive contains photographs of a substantial portion of the collection. These are the publication-quality plates from the original excavation report — lower resolution than a direct download but usable for typological work.

**Published corpus as image source:**
Brody's Mimbres Painted Pottery (1977) and Shafer's NAN Ranch Ruin (2003) both contain substantial photographic plates. A physical copy of either book, photographed with a phone under good light, produces uploadable images. Not ideal but functional.

---

### Practical Notes

Minimum useful image resolution: 1000px on the longest dimension. Larger is better.
The artifact page accepts up to 6 images — interior, exterior, profile, bottom, and detail views cover the most analytical ground for ceramic vessels.
Record the source URL and accession number in the artifact page fields when available. This builds a traceable research record.
