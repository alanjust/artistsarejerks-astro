## Reference Collections

### Peabody Museum of Archaeology and Ethnology, Harvard University
Holds the Swarts Ruin collection — one of the largest and most systematically documented assemblages of Classic Mimbres pottery. The Swarts excavation (1924–1927, Cosgrove & Cosgrove) produced hundreds of complete and restorable bowls with reliable provenience. Published in *The Swarts Ruin* (1932, Peabody Museum Papers). The Swarts collection is the primary reference class for Classic Mimbres Style III figurative bowls.

### Maxwell Museum of Anthropology, University of New Mexico
Major collection with strong regional focus. Well-documented provenience on most holdings. Accessible for comparative research.

### Smithsonian Institution (National Museum of Natural History)
Significant Mimbres holdings, including material from early excavations. Some provenience issues due to age of collection.

### Heard Museum, Phoenix
Strong Southwest collection with Mimbres representation. Well-presented contextual information.

### Mimbres Valley Field Sites (New Mexico)
Key documented sites (many heavily looted; provenience from looted material is compromised):

- **NAN Ranch Ruin** (Grant County, NM) — Most systematically excavated site. Shafer's work produced stratigraphically controlled data. Benchmark for production sequence and use-wear analysis.
- **Swarts Ruin** — Published assemblage; the Cosgrove report is still a primary typological reference.
- **Cameron Creek Village** — Excavated 1923–1928 (Bradfield). Good figurative assemblage.
- **Galaz Ruin** — Large site; Anyon and LeBlanc report (1984) important for social organization analysis.
- **Mattocks Site** — Early excavation; less controlled provenience but important figurative material.

## Canonical Vessel Types

### Classic Mimbres Style III figurative bowl
The reference object for the domain. Interior carries a figurative image — fish, rabbit, deer, human, composite being, or narrative scene — within a framing line system. Kill hole present or absent. White slip, mineral black paint, coil-built. Firing typically produces a hard, resonant vessel. Design field is the interior bowl surface; exterior is usually plain or minimally decorated.

The fish bowl is the most frequently encountered figurative type and functions as a baseline for image quality comparison — ubiquity means a wide range of execution quality exists in the corpus, from very high to routine.

### Classic Mimbres Style III geometric bowl
Same construction as figurative, but design program is geometric — interlocking scrolls, terraced triangles, hatching patterns, divided fields with bilateral or rotational symmetry. Often considered technically demanding because geometric precision is directly visible; errors read as errors rather than as expressive variation.

### Boldface Black-on-White (Style II)
Earlier than Style III (~1000–1050 CE). Bold geometric designs with thick lines, strong positive/negative contrast. Less refined surface preparation than Style III. Important for typological placement and reading the development sequence.

### Three Circle Red-on-White (Style I)
Late Pithouse period (~550–1000 CE). Red mineral paint on white or buff ground. Geometric, small-scale repetitive designs. Marks the beginning of the Mimbres decorated ceramic tradition. Vessels from this period are formally different from Classic Mimbres but establish the production tradition.

## Reference Publications as Canonical Objects

### Brody, J.J. — *Mimbres Painted Pottery* (1977, revised 2004)
The foundational catalogue and analysis. Plates are the primary visual reference corpus. Any specialist working on Mimbres imagery knows this book. Revised edition incorporates subsequent scholarship.

### Cosgrove, H.S. and C.B. — *The Swarts Ruin* (1932)
The excavation report for the Swarts site. Contains the systematic documentation of one of the largest controlled Mimbres assemblages. Typological and morphological descriptions are still used.

### Shafer, Harry J. — *Mimbres Archaeology at the NAN Ranch Ruin* (2003)
The production-analysis benchmark. Establishes what stratigraphically controlled Mimbres data looks like. Reference for use-wear analysis and production sequence interpretation.

### Anyon, Roger and Steven LeBlanc — *The Galaz Ruin* (1984)
Site report and social organization analysis. Reference for large-site demography and spatial organization.
