## Category context — Functional beverages

### What this category is

Functional beverages is a fast-moving, crowded category spanning: energy
drinks (Celsius, Ghost, Prime), electrolyte and hydration (Liquid I.V., LMNT,
Nuun), gut health and probiotics (Poppi, Olipop), adaptogen and nootropic drinks
(Recess, Kin Euphorics), non-alcoholic alternatives (Athletic Brewing, Ritual
Zero Proof), and better-for-you sodas.

The category is heavily driven by claims — what the product does for you —
and by ingredient credibility. Packaging has to communicate both quickly.

### Dominant visual conventions

**Color:** The category is bright. High-saturation pastels, neons, and bold
primary palettes dominate, particularly in energy and gut-health subcategories.
This is a deliberate break from the neutral/natural tones of traditional
wellness products and the dark palettes of conventional energy drinks (Monster,
Rockstar). Functional beverages positioned as "better for you" often use
colors that read as fun and approachable, not clinical.

**Background:** White and very light backgrounds are common — they signal
cleanliness, transparency, and better-for-you credentials. High contrast
between background and label graphics is a category norm.

**Typography:** Bold, modern sans-serifs dominate. Type is large — brands
understand this category lives on social media and e-commerce as much as
shelf. The wordmark has to read in a 200px-wide thumbnail.

**Claims and callouts:** Ingredient callouts are prominent and specific
("200mg caffeine," "1 billion probiotics," "only 35 calories"). These are
not regulatory boilerplate — they are active selling elements. Their hierarchy
matters. Ingredient transparency (showing what's actually in the product)
is a credibility signal used strategically.

**Illustration and imagery:** Character illustration, fruit/ingredient
photography, and abstract pattern are all common. Photography of the beverage
itself (liquid, bubbles, condensation) is used for appetite appeal. Abstract
or surreal illustration is used for adaptogen/nootropic brands aiming for
a more experiential, mood-focused positioning.

**Can vs. bottle:** Many brands in this category are canned. Can label
design requires different hierarchy consideration than a glass bottle —
the billboard face of a can is roughly 40% of the circumference. Key
information must read on that face without wrapping.

### Where category conventions break down

The functional beverage category is actively producing counter-conventions
as it matures:
- LMNT uses muted, minimal packaging against the bright category norm —
  signaling seriousness and performance credibility to a fitness-focused buyer
- Athletic Brewing uses craft beer visual language to signal quality within
  NA beer, not functional beverage language
- Kin Euphorics uses dark, moody, nightlife-adjacent visuals for its
  adaptogen drinks — borrowing from spirits to signal adult sophistication

Assess whether a package is deliberately disrupting category norms or is
simply not conforming because of poor category awareness.

## Category reference class — Functional beverages

These replace the generic CPG reference class from the base block for this
category.

**Benchmark performers**

**Olipop** — gut-health soda. Vintage illustration style, warm color palette,
clear flavor communication, nostalgic positioning against the better-for-you
platform. The illustration approach became highly distinctive in a category
that skewed toward modern/clinical. Strong trial driver. Benchmark for
personality-forward differentiation in functional beverages.

**Poppi** — prebiotic soda. Bright colors, fruit-forward photography,
playful wordmark. Performs on social-first discovery — the packaging reads
well as a thumbnail. Benchmark for e-commerce and social-optimized design
in this category.

**Liquid I.V.** — hydration multiplier. Clear stick-pack format with
visible ingredient callouts. The "multiplier" claim is carried visually
through bold typography. Benchmark for claim-forward hierarchy and
ingredient credibility communication.

**Celsius** — energy. Evolved from a clinical/medical look to a more
lifestyle-forward visual system. Category-dominant through distinctive
color variants per flavor. Benchmark for color-as-variant-navigation
in a complex range.

**Athletic Brewing** — non-alcoholic craft beer. Uses craft beer visual
language deliberately — label illustration style, brewery photography,
hop-forward imagery — rather than functional beverage conventions.
Succeeds by being the anti-functional-beverage functional beverage.
Benchmark for category borrowing as positioning strategy.

**LMNT** — electrolytes. Deliberately minimal against a loud category.
Muted color palette, no illustration, clinical ingredient focus. Serves
a performance/fitness audience that reads "serious" as a quality signal.
Benchmark for intentional category disruption through restraint.

**Counter-case**

**Early Monster Energy** — the original dark/aggressive energy drink
visual language that functional beverages have largely moved away from.
Use as a reference for what the category is actively differentiating from,
not as a benchmark.

## Category routing notes

**Commercial objective:** This category is heavily trial-driven. Most
functional beverage brands are relatively new and are competing for first
purchase. "Drive trial" is the default objective unless there is a specific
loyalty or repositioning brief.

**Brand stage:** The functional beverage category is young — most brands
are new entrants with limited established assets. Evaluate new entrant
criteria unless the brand has 5+ years of consistent visual identity and
documented market presence.

**Channel matters:** Functional beverages sell through multiple channels
with different visual contexts. A package optimized for Whole Foods shelf
may read differently than one optimized for Amazon thumbnail or gym vending
machine. Note the primary channel in the routing step if known.

**Social media presence:** More than almost any other CPG category,
functional beverage packaging is designed for social media as much as
shelf. A package that photographs well and reads at small sizes has an
advantage the evaluation should note.
