# Four-Domain Landing Page Copy

---

You love art. Or you make it.

Use this tool to read a work — your own or someone else's. By the end, what you think you know will look different.

Almost certainly.

It reads across four dimensions you've been responding to all along — but probably never had language for.

Here's what each read uncovers.

---

## What your eyes do before you decide anything—Perceptual

In the first seconds of looking — before you name what you're seeing or form an opinion — your nervous system is already tracking. Where attention goes first. What holds it. What lets it go. What the work is asking you to do visually. Most analysis of art skips straight past this. This doesn't.

---

## Whether what it's made of works with what it's doing—Materialness

You've probably felt this without being able to name it. A photograph inkjet-printed on shiny canvas stretched over bars — something's off, but you can't quite say what. That's a material register problem. When material and intent are working together, the work has a physical coherence that most viewers feel without knowing why. This register names both.

---

## Where it lands—Cultural

A Mondrian pattern means something different now than it did in 1921. Not because the work changed—but because a century of notebooks, tote bags, and hotel lobbies got between you and it.

That's the cultural register. Who showed it. Who bought it. What owning it signals—sometimes love, sometimes status, sometimes both. This read maps what's been layered onto a work since it left the studio. And what's left once you see the layers.

---

## What conversation it's already in—Conceptual

Abstract Expressionism wasn't just a style. It was a position — about consciousness, about gesture, about what painting could mean. Most work is in dialogue with ideas whether the maker named them or not. If you've never thought about your work in those terms, that's fine. This register finds the conversation you've entered. From there, you decide what to do with it.
